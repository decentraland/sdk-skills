# Visuals & captures reference

## Captures

- **A `screenshot` is the frame the scene is showing, not a stale one.** The capture waits out a camera blend first (up to 1.5 s — an SDK virtual camera handing back, a mode switch — and the caption says ", waited N ms for the camera blend", or that it was still blending at the deadline; `waitForCameraBlend: false` opts out), and `reload_scene` returns only after the reloaded scene has ticked and rendered, so a capture right after either shows the new state rather than the previous frame. A capture costs a few frames (HiDPI screens included), so re-shooting "after a beat" is no longer a workaround to carry.
- **Sub-second events are `capture_sequence`, never repeated screenshots.** `count` frames `intervalSec` apart (down to 0.05 s) are captured in-process at the true interval and written as files under the app's temporary cache (`.../mcp-captures/<stamp>/frame-NNN.jpg`, never a scene folder); the result lists each frame's path and timestamp, the mean interval and how many frames ran `late`, with no image inline — read only the frames you need. Default width 640 px keeps a burst cheap.
- **One capture at a time.** `screenshot` and `capture_sequence` share one gate: two `screenshot`s in one tool batch collide and the second fails with "A capture is already in progress (screenshot); retry when it completes" — re-issue it alone. A `screenshot` batched *after* a `capture_sequence` is serialised behind it and simply succeeds; the refusal naming `capture_sequence` reaches only a call fired out of band while the sequence runs.
- `scripts/screenshot.sh -n N -i SEC` is `capture_sequence` from the shell (chunks of 20, the tool's maximum per call), copying the frames to `$TMPDIR/mcp-shots`; `-h` prints the flags.

## Visual tuning

- `screenshot`'s `worldOnly` (and the script's `--world-only`) renders with full post-processing, bloom included. Emissive/glow looks are judgeable from world-only frames, with one caveat: halo spread reads slightly tighter than the full-view capture (render-resolution difference), so do final pixel-exact glow tuning on full-view frames.
- Neon emissive recipe (with bloom, against a sunset skybox): pin one emissive channel to exactly 0 (e.g. `(1, 0, 0.6)` magenta) — a small minor channel (0.15-0.2) times `emissiveIntensity` 6+ whites the whole surface out. Then weight by luminance and background: green-heavy hues (cyan) bloom to white far faster than magenta and additively desaturate against the pink haze (cyan + pink = white), so dim or blue-shift them (`(0, 0.35, 0.8)` works); magenta reinforces the sunset and stays saturated at intensity 6-15. A large on-screen emitter always whites out at its core — the hue lives in the halo, which is what a real neon tube looks like; judge hue from gameplay distance, not close-ups. Bloom already provides the halo, so an emitter needs no alpha-blended "glow shell" box around it — a shell just reads as a grey display case.
- UiBackground texture entities render far stronger than a near-zero `color` alpha suggests (a 0.05-alpha noise overlay fully obscures the image beneath). For flicker/glitch overlays, mount/unmount the entity conditionally (`{cond ? <UiEntity .../> : null}`) instead of animating alpha near zero.
- Thin geometry (a door panel ~0.1m) is invisible edge-on: a panel "open" at ~90-110° reads as a sliver from the front. Cross-examine pose via ECS rotation (`get_entity_details` quaternion) as well as pixels, and prefer open angles like ~135° when front visibility matters.
- `SkyboxTime.create(engine.RootEntity, { fixedTime })` pins the scene to a permanent time of day regardless of launch flags.
