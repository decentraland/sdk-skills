# Interaction testing reference

Read before the first click, hover, key press, or scene-UI call of a session. Field findings from driving real scenes — most of these are silent failures, not errors.

## Clicking and aiming

- **Two id spaces, and the tools use the one your scene's logs do not print.** `entityId` on `click_entity`/`get_entity_details` and every id from `list_scene_entities` are the *engine's* entity ids; the `crdtEntityId` in click/hover results, and every id a scene logs itself (`engine.addEntity()` values printed by its own `console.log`), are *CRDT* ids. They are different numbers for the same object, neither is a range you can recognise, and pasting a scene-logged id into `click_entity` silently clicks a different entity — which is how a run ended up toggling the wrong panel twice. Map one to the other with `get_entity_details <engine id>`: its dump lists `CRDTEntity` alongside the components, so it names both ids for that object. Address by world point (`x/y/z`) when you know where a thing is and only have its CRDT id.
- `click_entity` presses a pointer button on a scene entity (get ids from `list_scene_entities`). The target needs a `PointerEvents` component and a collider; the aim is validated by a real camera-origin raycast, so an occluder **that has no `PointerEvents` of its own** returns `hit:false` + `blockedBy*` (reposition and retry; a clickable occluder reads as a hit instead — next bullet) and the entity's `maxDistance` (default 10 m) applies — get close first. `upRayMissed: true` means the release did not reach the target (the target moved, or something slid into the line — e.g. a door starting to swing): the scene received the press and **no** release, so a scene that tracks held pointers is left holding one until it times out on its own. For GLTF entities whose collider sits away from the pivot, pass an explicit `x/y/z` aim point. The player must be standing on the scene's parcel — off-parcel clicks fail with "no running current scene".
- **`maxDistance` is measured to where the camera ray *enters* the collider, so the gate moves with the camera pose.** In third person the number compared against the entity's `maxDistance` (and reported as `distance`) runs from an avatar-height point on the player to the hit point of the camera-origin ray — not to the aim point, not to the entity's origin, and not the player-to-target distance you computed from positions; in first person it is the camera ray's own length. Where the ray enters the collider depends on where the camera sits, so a stale orbit makes the same target read further away: `hover_entity` on a `maxDistance: 2` target from ~1.8 m failed with "out of range (hit distance 2.08m)", and the identical call after a `look_at` at the target hit at **1.22 m**. `look_at` the target before any click, hover or press against a tight gate — it parks the camera behind the player on the line to the target, so the ray enters the near face — and read a failure's `hit distance` as the ray's number, not as proof the player stands too far away.
- **An occluder that is itself clickable does not report as an occluder — it reports as a hit.** `blockedBy*` appears only when the thing in the way has no `PointerEvents`; a nearer entity that *does* have them is accepted as the target of any aim that has no `entityId` to check against, so the call returns `hit:true` for the wrong entity and fires its handler. Observed: an aim at the S9 toggle (`2394,1,2400`) returned `hit:true` `crdtEntityId:581` — the S8 toggle, 1 m short of the aim point — and toggled the S8 panel closed. So **always compare the returned `crdtEntityId` with the one you aimed at**; it is the only channel that tells you whether you hit your target. **Passing `entityId` is what stops it — but only on a client built after 2026-09-03.** An aim with an `entityId` is delivered to that entity or to nobody: the posted edge names the entity, and the pipeline withholds it from anything else the ray reached (and from the scene root). **That guarantee covers `entityId` aims only.** A refusal on an `x/y/z`-only aim, or a `click_at` miss, still posts one `PET_DOWN` to the scene **root** (measured 5/5: out-of-range, occlusion-blocked, no-collider and two ray misses) — an unqualified edge belongs to the root, so a scene's global counters climb while you drive click negative cases. Since 2026-09-05 the matching `PET_UP` follows on a later tick and the result says `rootBroadcast: true`, so a miss no longer leaves anything held. **On an older build the `PET_UP` never comes** — the click stopped after a press that did not hit — and the orphaned hold is not inert: a scene tracking a held button on the root samples `PrimaryPointerInfo`, whose ray follows the camera, so every `look_at`, `move_to` or `camera_look` you issue inside the scene's timeout window drives a gesture you never made (observed: a refused click followed by a repositioning `look_at` painted a stroke). There, treat a `hit:false` on an `x/y/z` aim as a live press until the scene's timeout has passed, or send the release yourself (`click_entity eventType:"up"` at the same aim). Older builds check the target only *after* posting, so there `hit:false` with `blockedByCrdtId` still fires the blocker's handler — measured: two such "misses" produced two panel toggles. If you cannot tell which build you are on, treat a `hit:false` naming an interactable blocker as "may have clicked it" and re-read scene state. Either way a refusal still produces `PetHoverEnter`/`PetHoverLeave` on whatever the ray reached — a real cursor passing over an occluder does the same — so a scene counting hovers will see them.
- **`look_at` the aim point immediately before every `click_entity`/`hover_entity`.** Clicks and hovers are camera-origin rays, so a stale orbit yaw from an earlier `look_at` can put an unrelated entity in the line and the click lands on the wrong target (observed: hit a neighbouring box 4 m off-axis instead of the intended one). `move_to(lookAt…)` is not a substitute: it is the unrefined production look-at where it works at all, and on which builds it works is in [`camera-and-movement.md`](camera-and-movement.md).
- **To drag a held pointer across the world, use `sweep_pointer` — not `ui_drag`.** It presses a button on a target (`entityId` or `x/y/z`), turns the camera while it is held (`deltaX`/`deltaY`/`seconds`, same units as `camera_look`), then releases. That is the gesture a human makes, and the only one that drags the ray a scene samples from `PrimaryPointerInfo`: the press parks the pointer on the target's pixel and the camera turn then drags the ray through that pixel across the world, while a synthetic *aim* (`click_at`/`click_entity`) does not move it at all. **`look_at` the target immediately before the sweep** — only a press that lands *on screen* parks the pointer (a world aim needs no line of sight, so a press on something behind you still reports `hit`), and with nothing parked the camera turns and no ray is dragged. Read `pressed.hit` too — a press that landed on nothing armed nothing. The press needs a target with `PointerEvents` in range, exactly like `click_entity`.
- **Your calls are serialised: a held `press_input` issued together with a `camera_look` holds the button with the camera pinned, then turns after the release** (measured out-of-band with a detached yaw poller: yaw pinned at `29.73` across 60+ samples during a 2 s hold, the turn beginning 2.4 s after release; the scene painted one dot). `sweep_pointer` holds the press, the turn and the release inside one call, which is why it exists. Concurrency between your own calls is unmeasurable from inside them — both results land in the same batch, so a yaw read before and after *looks* like a turn during the hold, and a poller issued in that batch is serialised too. Only a **detached background poller** started before the gesture (raw JSON-RPC `get_player_state` on a ~135 ms loop) separates the two; without one, make no claim about concurrency.
- `hover_entity` holds the reticle on an entity without clicking (`PetHoverEnter`/`PetHoverLeave` fire like a real cursor) and reports the hover tooltip text — use it to verify hover feedback before committing to a click.

## Input actions

- `press_input` sends any SDK input action (primary/secondary/action_3…) with the real-key fan-out. Pass `entityId` or an `x/y/z` aim point to land it entity-bound; the result then reports the target, distance and hover text like a click does. It does NOT move the player — combine with `walk` when a scene expects movement keys.
- **An unaimed `press_input` binds to whatever the client's cursor pixel rests over — anywhere on screen, wherever the human left it — and `look_at` does not control that.** Observed: `look_at` on a wall with no `PointerEvents`, then an unaimed `press_input action:forward`, returned `entityBound: true` on an unrelated button **15.87 m away**, suppressing the root broadcast the step existed to count while firing no handler (the action was not in that entity's `pointerEvents`); another bound to a toggle **~62° off the camera's forward axis**. Aim explicitly (`entityId` or `x/y/z`) whenever you want a specific target. To clear the reticle for a root broadcast, pitch the view into empty sky with `camera_look deltaY:8 seconds:1.5` — measured 4/4 it took two calls (about -26° then about -53°, the third-person clamp), so read the returned `cameraRotationEuler.x` (Unity eulers: `351.82` is -8°, `306` is -54°) and repeat until the view is empty. **`entityBound: false` is an intention, not a receipt**: 14 unaimed presses across 7 actions, 3 scene lifetimes, both camera modes and 3 parcels all returned it plus the "delivered to the scene root" hint while the scene received nothing (a client regression fixed 2026-09-03; the reporting gap is permanent). Confirm a root broadcast in the **scene's own channel** — `PointerEventsResult.get(engine.RootEntity)` with a timestamp watermark.
- **A half-readable aim is refused, and a rejected number says what it was.** `press_input` used to treat an unusable `x/y/z` as *no aim*, so one fumbled coordinate turned an entity-bound press into a scene-root broadcast that still read as success, and `click_entity`/`hover_entity`/`sweep_pointer` fell back to the entity's centre the same way; all four now refuse an `x/y/z` that is not three numbers. Every tool that requires numbers names the offender and what arrived — `... (y arrived as string "3.0", not a number)` — so read the error rather than re-deriving it: it is telling you your argument was not a number, not that you forgot it. A whole number written `N.0` has been seen arriving that way; write `3`, not `3.0`. **That coercion happens in your MCP client, before the request exists, so it does not reproduce over `curl` (where `3.0` stays a number) — a miscased `action` reproduces from any client, because the mistake is in the value you chose, not in how it was carried.** `action` values are lowercase and matched exactly (`primary`, `action_3` — never `PRIMARY`, `IA_PRIMARY` or `action3`), and the refusal names what arrived: `action does not accept string "PRIMARY"; the values are lowercase, one of: …` (before 2026-09-05 it said `action is required`, which reads as a forgotten argument and invites you to re-send the same value). Every enum argument answers the same way — `action`, `mode`, `stack`, `path`, `button`, `kind`, `eventType`, `severity`, `quality`. Both rejections come from the server and both echo your value, so they look alike in a transcript; tell them apart by comparing the echo with what you typed: `string "3.0"` where you wrote a number is your client's transport, `string "PRIMARY"` where you wrote `PRIMARY` is you.
- **The fan-out is either/or: an edge that lands entity-bound never also broadcasts to the scene root** (verified in-world at the CRDT level). An aimed `press_input`/`click_entity`/`click_at` on a qualified target produces only the entity result; unaimed (or unqualified) edges reach the scene root instead. The suppression is per scene update: a second action whose edge lands in the same update as an entity-bound one loses its broadcast too — unreachable from your serialised calls, so only a human at the keyboard can trigger it. **The measurement trap that faked the opposite three runs straight: `inputSystem.isTriggered` cannot read the scene root in ANY form.** Without an entity it scans every entity's `PointerEventsResult`; passing `engine.RootEntity` does the exact same thing, because the root entity is `0` and the SDK's `if (entity)` guard treats it as absent (JavaScript falsy zero — same bug in `getInputCommand`). A scene must read `PointerEventsResult.get(engine.RootEntity)` directly, with a timestamp watermark, to observe the root broadcast. Check the scene's measurement before blaming the client — and beware a counter that only increments inside an active state (an "off-canvas samples" that needs a stroke to be open first), because it stays at zero both when nothing was sampled and when everything sampled missed. Two live runs drew opposite-but-wrong client conclusions from exactly that.

## Movement

- `walk` moves relative to the camera and requires an explicit direction: pass `directionY: 1` for forward (`directionX` strafes); omitting both errors with "directionX and directionY must not both be zero". Scene `InputModifier` movement locks apply to `walk` exactly as they do to WASD — a zero-distance result inside a freeze zone proves the lock works; pass `ignoreInputModifiers: true` to escape deliberately.
- **`move_to` is a teleport and scenes notice.** It fires the scene's movement observers as a superhuman burst (e.g. `distance=16m over 0.05s avgSpeed=320m/s`), and a teleport that leaves one trigger volume and enters another can deliver `enter(B)` **before** `exit(A)` — B's exit handler then clears state A just installed, so a scene-wide `InputModifier` silently ends up cleared while the player stands inside the zone that set it. Use `walk` when the scene watches movement or trigger boundaries, and re-enter a zone on foot before trusting its state.
- Collider checks beat pixels for physics (cross-examine): `look_at` straight at the target, `walk` forward, then compare `get_player_state` positions to prove passage or blockage.

## Scene UI (`ReactEcsRenderer` panels)

- `ui_list stack:sdk` lists the interactable elements with their CRDT ids and screen rects; `ui_click`/`ui_set_text` fire the same pointer/value/submit events a user produces. A covered element fails with `blockedBy` instead of clicking through — that's the occlusion check working, not a bug.
- **A `disabled` element is refused, not written to.** `ui_set_text` on a disabled `<Input />` (or `ui_click` on a disabled element) fails naming the disabled state, because a user cannot type into or click one — the write would otherwise fire the scene's `onChange` for input the scene declared impossible. `ui_list` marks these `disabled: true`, so check the listing before blaming the tool. `force` does not override it: `force` skips the occlusion check, and there is nothing to skip on an inert element.
- **A written `<Input />` value and the box you see can disagree — trust the scene, never the pixels.** The write reaches the scene's `onChange` (logs and echo labels confirm the value), but the rendered field is a separate buffer the scene only repaints when its own `value` prop changes: fields the scene passes a `value` to display the write and then keep the *old* text after the scene clears them (three fields, `read back: ""` while the boxes still showed `overwritten` / `delta-9` / `77123`). Verify text entry from the scene's own channel — `get_scene_logs`, `get_entity_details`, an echo label — and do not report "the field shows X" from a screenshot.
- **`submit: true` empties the box, and that is the submit working.** The client blanks an SDK input the moment it has handed the submit to the scene — exactly what it does when a human presses Enter — so an empty field after `ui_set_text submit:true` means the value *was* delivered, not that the write was swallowed. Read the value back from the scene, never from the field, and do not go looking for a client bug here: an uncontrolled field renders a plain `ui_set_text` write fine, and nothing but a submit clears one.
- **An invisible client panel can eat your click, and its reach is not predictable from a screenshot — or from the last session.** The chat message feed's viewport is fully transparent but still takes clicks, stays active in the chat's normal state even with no messages, and sorts above scene UI, so "the screenshot shows nothing there" is not evidence of a clear pixel. How far up it reaches varies between sessions: one run had a `ui_click` at normalized `y 0.5366` blocked by it, and the next run clicked two elements at that exact height plain, no `force`, with the chat collapsed ("Press Enter to chat") both times (the sessions ran at different screen sizes, 3424x1926 vs 2188x1231). So do not pre-empt it: issue the plain click and read the result. A failure with `blockedBy: ".../ChatMessages/Viewport"` is the check working — a real click there focuses the chat — and the answer is to move the element, or pass `force: true` deliberately for that one call; a plain success means nothing covered the pixel this time, not that the cover is gone.
- **`click_at` cannot address UI — it rays into the 3D world** and reports the scene *entity* it hit. Clicking a UI element is `ui_click`, addressed by `crdtId` (sdk) or `path`/`id` (ugui), never by screen position. `ui_drag` is the one UI tool that takes screen coordinates. A `click_at` on a pixel covered by client UI or the scene's own UI **fails** with that cover in `blockedByUi` rather than clicking the world behind it — that is the check working, the same as `ui_click`'s; pass `force: true` only when you deliberately want the world through an overlay. When the cover is scene UI, `blockedByUi` names its `crdtId` ("the scene's UI (crdtId 598)") — feed that straight to `ui_click`; a client-UI cover names the element's path instead.
- **Feed `ui_drag` an element's `center`; never divide its `screenRect` by the screenshot size.** `screenRect` is in the client's *screen* pixels, which on a HiDPI display are far larger than the image you looked at (rects ran ~3424x1926 while `screenshot` returned 1280x720). Every listing and UI action result states that space as `screen: {width, height}` — on every result, device path and failures included — and hands you a normalized `center` alongside the rect whenever an element was resolved — use the `center`, and when you need a point that isn't one, normalize against `screen`, not against the capture. A `screenshot` caption states the screen size too whenever it downscaled.
- **Scene UI events arrive one per drain window, so `ui_click`/`ui_drag` take several frames.** The scene's pointer-event slot (`UITransformComponent.PointerEventTriggered`) holds a single event drained by a throttled scene system, and the tools wait for each event to be consumed before sending the next (enter → down → up → leave) — `onMouseDown` *and* `onMouseUp` handlers both fire. A `ui_drag` starting inside the scene UI is delivered to its elements semantically (press on the start element, release on the end element; result says `path: "sdk"`). An unconsumed press or release **fails** the call ("the scene did not consume the release") rather than reporting a delivery that never happened, so a success result is evidence of delivery; still verify a drag by its effect when the stakes are high.
- **`ui_drag` picks its path per call — pass `path:"sdk"` when you mean the scene's UI.** With the default `path:"auto"` it runs semantically (result `path:"sdk"`) only when the current scene has a UI panel *and* the start point picks an element on it; otherwise it replays the virtual mouse (`path:"device"`), which for a start point over scene UI means the drag goes into the 3D world instead (observed: it painted a world stroke). Byte-identical calls have taken different paths seconds apart — the scene UI can still be attaching or laying out. A fallback is no longer silent: the result carries `pathReason` saying why the semantic path did not apply, and `ok:true` there means the *mouse* states were replayed, not that the UI got it — `pointerOver` on that result names what each end of the drag was actually over. `path:"sdk"` fails the call with that reason instead of dragging the world, which is what you want whenever the target is scene UI.
- **`device` belongs to `ui_click` only; `ui_drag` selects its path with `path`.** `ui_drag device:true` used to be dropped without a word and the drag ran on `auto`; since 2026-09-05 any argument a tool does not declare is refused by name (`ui_drag has no argument 'device'; its arguments are: …`), so an unfamiliar rejection listing the tool's arguments means a key of yours belongs to a sibling tool — not that the tool is broken.
- **The virtual-device path (`ui_click device:true`, `ui_drag path:"device"`) drives the client UI stack, never UI Toolkit scene panels** — an SDK element reports that it observed nothing, so use the semantic path for `stack:sdk` and the device path for `stack:ugui`. A device drag verifies no target: `ok` means the mouse states were replayed, so read `pointerOver: {start, end}` — it names what covered each end of the drag, or `"world"` when nothing did, plus an `info` line when both ends are `"world"` (reporting that landed 2026-09-03; a bare `ok:true` with no `pointerOver` means an older build, not a different behavior). Over the world it painted no stroke and did not move the camera (measured twice on the S10 stroke canvas, 20 and 45 frames, cursor `Free`, yaw pinned `359.98`); since the left button is also the camera-pan binding, the same call can instead fail with "the drag panned the camera instead of dragging", and a locked cursor fails any pointer gesture up front ("pointer gestures need a free cursor") — client cursor state you don't control. Sweep a held pointer across the world with `sweep_pointer`.
- `ui_scroll`'s `dy` follows image coordinates — **positive scrolls the content down**. The result reports the offset actually achieved, so "did not move" means a clamp (already at that end, or the content does not overflow), not a broken call.

## Trigger areas

- Trigger areas fire `onTriggerEnter` immediately after `reload_scene` if the player is already standing inside one — reposition the player outside all triggers before testing enter/exit sequencing (and treat post-reload trigger logs as stale state, not gameplay).
- **Thin `TriggerArea` volumes never fire.** A 0.1 m-tall slab (a floor plate or zone marker) does not overlap the avatar capsule standing on top of it. Keep the thin box as the visual and put the trigger in a separate invisible box ~2 m tall centred at capsule height.
