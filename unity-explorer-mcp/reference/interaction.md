# Interaction testing reference

Read before the first click, hover, key press, or scene-UI call of a session. Most of these are silent failures, not errors.

## Clicking and aiming

- **Two id spaces, and the tools use the one your scene's logs do not print.** `entityId` on `click_entity`/`get_entity_details` and every id from `list_scene_entities` are the *engine's* entity ids; the `crdtEntityId` in click/hover results, and every id a scene logs itself (`engine.addEntity()` values printed by its own `console.log`), are *CRDT* ids. They are different numbers for the same object, neither is a range you can recognise, and pasting a scene-logged id into `click_entity` silently clicks a different entity. Map one to the other with `list_scene_entities crdtEntity:<crdt id>`, which returns that entity's engine `id` in one call (every listed entry carries both `id` and `crdtEntity`), or with `get_entity_details <engine id>`, whose dump lists `CRDTEntity` alongside the components. Address by world point (`x/y/z`) when you know where a thing is and only have its CRDT id.
- `click_entity` presses a pointer button on a scene entity (get ids from `list_scene_entities`). **The default button is `pointer` — the left click, `IA_POINTER`.** A handler bound to E or F (`InputAction.IA_PRIMARY` / `IA_SECONDARY`) never fires from it: pass `button: primary` / `secondary`, or `press_input action:primary` with the `entityId`. Every pointer result names the `button` it sent, and `hit:true` with a `warning` (one part per edge: `press: …; release: …`) saying *the entity declares no PointerEvents entry for pet_down/pointer; it listens for: …* means the aim was right but no declared handler ran — fix the button, not the aim. The entity still received a raw `PointerEventsResult` for the button you sent (`pointerEventsSystem` callbacks bound to another button ignore it; `inputSystem.isTriggered(IA_POINTER, PET_DOWN, entity)` sees it), and the scene root got **no** broadcast: a result written to any entity suppresses it, exactly as for a human's wrong-button click — so a root counter that stays put is the expected reading, not a lost edge. The target needs a `PointerEvents` component and a collider; the aim is validated by a real camera-origin raycast, so an occluder **that has no `PointerEvents` of its own** returns `hit:false` + `blockedBy*` (reposition and retry; a clickable occluder reads as a hit instead — next bullet) and the entity's `maxDistance` (default 10 m, measured from the **avatar**, not the camera) applies — move the avatar close first; a camera that can see the target is not enough. `upRayMissed: true` means the release did not reach the target (the target moved, or something slid into the line — e.g. a door starting to swing): the scene received the press and **no** release, so a scene that tracks held pointers is left holding one until it times out on its own. For GLTF entities whose collider sits away from the pivot, pass an explicit `x/y/z` aim point. The player must be standing on the scene's parcel — off-parcel clicks fail with "no running current scene".
- **The `maxDistance` gate moves with the camera pose.** It is the avatar's distance to where the camera-origin ray enters the collider, so a stale orbit can refuse a target the player stands well within. `look_at` the target before any click, hover or press against a tight gate — it parks the camera behind the player on the line to the target, so the ray enters the near face.
- **`hit` / `out of range` is the gate's verdict; the reported `distance` is context, never the gated number.** The `distance` in a click/hover result, and the `hit distance` in a refusal, are the camera-to-hit ray length, so they can exceed `maxDistance` on a *successful* call — a `maxDistance: 2` target can hit while reporting several metres. Assert on `hit`, and read a refusal's number as the ray's length, not as proof the player stands too far away.
- **An occluder that is itself clickable does not report as an occluder — it reports as a hit.** `blockedBy*` appears only when the thing in the way has no `PointerEvents`; a nearer entity that *does* have them is accepted as the target of any aim that has no `entityId` to check against, so the call returns `hit:true` for the wrong entity and fires its handler — an aim at a button with another button a metre in front of it clicks the front one. So **always compare the returned `crdtEntityId` with the one you aimed at**; it is the only channel that tells you whether you hit your target.
- **Passing `entityId` is what stops the wrong-target hit.** An aim with an `entityId` is delivered to that entity or to nobody: the posted edge names the entity, and the pipeline withholds it from anything else the ray reached (and from the scene root).
- **A refused `x/y/z` aim still reaches the scene root.** A refusal on an `x/y/z`-only aim, or a `click_at` miss — out of range, occlusion-blocked, no collider, or a plain ray miss — posts one `PET_DOWN` to the scene **root**, and the result says `rootBroadcast: true`: an unqualified edge belongs to the root, so a scene's global counters climb while you drive click negative cases. The matching `PET_UP` follows on a later tick, so a miss leaves nothing held. A refusal also produces `PetHoverEnter`/`PetHoverLeave` on whatever the ray reached — a real cursor passing over an occluder does the same — so a scene counting hovers will see them.
- **`look_at` the aim point immediately before every `click_entity`/`hover_entity`.** Clicks and hovers are camera-origin rays, so a stale orbit yaw from an earlier `look_at` can put an unrelated entity in the line and the click lands on the wrong target. `move_to` with `lookAt*` ends on the same refinement, so it aims as well when the move itself is wanted (see [`camera-and-movement.md`](camera-and-movement.md)). **The pose that aims best photographs worst**, so plan the verification separately from the aim: that same `look_at` lines the third-person camera up *through* the avatar, which then occludes the very target you framed. Verify an interaction from the scene's own channel — `get_scene_logs`, `get_entity_details` — or `move_to` a mark offset sideways from the camera→target line before capturing.
- **To drag a held pointer across the world, use `sweep_pointer` — not `ui_drag`.** It presses a button on a target (`entityId` or `x/y/z`), turns the camera while it is held (`deltaX`/`deltaY`/`seconds`, same units as `camera_look` — and how far a unit turns varies several-fold between machines, so size the sweep from a measured pan, see [`camera-and-movement.md`](camera-and-movement.md)), then releases. That is the gesture a human makes, and the only one that drags the ray a scene samples from `PrimaryPointerInfo`: the press parks the pointer on the target's pixel and the camera turn then drags the ray through that pixel across the world, while a synthetic *aim* (`click_at`/`click_entity`) does not move it at all. **`look_at` the target immediately before the sweep** — only a press that lands *on screen* parks the pointer (a world aim needs no line of sight, so a press on something behind you still reports `hit`), and with nothing parked the camera turns and no ray is dragged. Read `pressed.hit` too — a press that landed on nothing armed nothing. The press needs a target with `PointerEvents` in range, exactly like `click_entity`.
- **Your calls are serialised: a held `press_input` issued together with a `camera_look` holds the button with the camera pinned, then turns after the release.** `sweep_pointer` holds the press, the turn and the release inside one call, which is why it exists. Concurrency between your own calls is unmeasurable from inside them — both results land in the same batch, so a yaw read before and after *looks* like a turn during the hold, and a poller issued in that batch is serialised too. Only a **detached background poller** started before the gesture (raw JSON-RPC `get_player_state` on a ~135 ms loop) separates the two; without one, make no claim about concurrency. `screenshot` is the tool that does *not* queue this way — see the one-capture-per-batch rule in SKILL.md.
- **`get_reticle` is the one-call "can a player find this by aiming" check.** It reads what the reticle ray reaches right now — `kind` `scene_entity` (with `crdtEntityId`, `hoverText`, `withinRange` of its `PointerEvents`, `hoverQualified`), `avatar`, bare `world` geometry or `nothing` — with the ray and the distances, and sends the scene nothing. The ray goes through the screen centre while the cursor is locked and through the cursor when it is free, exactly as for a human, so `look_at` (or `camera_look`) first, then read it: a prompt that `click_entity` reaches by id but `get_reticle` never names is one a player cannot find. It errors while the cursor is panning or the in-world camera is open. **The `cursor` can lie outside `screen`** — the OS cursor is off the window, the normal state when the Explorer runs inside the Unity Editor or you drive it from a terminal: `cursor.onScreen` is then false, `center` is clamped to the edge, and the ray still goes through the off-screen pixel, so `kind` names what that off-screen ray reaches (it reached a CLEAR button behind the player once). Never feed that clamped `center` to `click_at`; lock the cursor (then the ray is the screen centre `look_at` aimed) or aim by `x/y/z`.
- **`get_visible_entities` answers "is it in frame" without an image.** It lists the scene's rendered entities inside the camera frustum, largest on screen first, each with `screenRect` (full-resolution screen pixels, origin top-left) and a normalized `center` in the stated `screen`, `coverage`, and distances. **Occlusion is not evaluated** — an entity behind a wall is still listed and a bounds box is larger than its mesh — so it proves framing and catches something filling the view (a body at 0.75 m), never visibility through walls; avatars are not included.
- `hover_entity` holds the reticle on an entity without clicking (`PetHoverEnter`/`PetHoverLeave` fire like a real cursor) and reports the hover tooltip text — use it to verify hover feedback before committing to a click. **A refused hover reports `rootBroadcast: true` as well, and unlike a refused click it moves no counter.** The field is not click-only — an out-of-range hover carries it — but what reaches the root is a hover edge, not the `PET_DOWN` a refused click posts: the scene's pointer counters read unchanged straight after, and nothing is left armed. Read it as *this edge went to the root*, and reserve the leak accounting above for click misses.

## Input actions

- `press_input` sends any SDK input action (primary/secondary/action_3…) with the real-key fan-out. Pass `entityId` or an `x/y/z` aim point to land it entity-bound; the result then reports the target, distance and hover text like a click does. It does NOT move the player — combine with `walk` when a scene expects movement keys.
- **An unaimed `press_input` binds to whatever the client's cursor pixel rests over — anywhere on screen, wherever the human left it — and `look_at` does not control that.** It can bind to an interactable many metres away and tens of degrees off the camera's axis, even right after a `look_at` onto geometry with no `PointerEvents`, suppressing the root broadcast you meant to send while firing no handler when the action is not in that entity's `pointerEvents`. A preceding aimed gesture does not reliably leave the cursor on its target either, so an unaimed press or hold after it may or may not land on that target. **A cursor outside the window still counts**: a human's click there reaches another window, but the synthetic edge binds through the off-screen ray (measured: an unaimed hold bound to a scene's CLEAR button and wiped its canvas), and the result then carries a `warning` naming the off-window cursor — read it before reading the scene. Aim explicitly (`entityId` or `x/y/z`) whenever you want a specific target. To clear the reticle for a root broadcast, pitch the view into empty sky with `camera_look deltaY:8 seconds:1.5`, then **read the returned `cameraRotationEuler.x` and repeat until the view is empty** (Unity eulers: `351.82` is -8°, `306` is -54°, the third-person clamp). It can take one call or several, so check the value, don't count calls. **`entityBound: false` is an intention, not a receipt**: it says where the press was sent, not that the scene received it. Confirm a root broadcast in the **scene's own channel** — `PointerEventsResult.get(engine.RootEntity)` with a timestamp watermark.
- **A half-readable aim is refused, and a rejected number says what it was.** `press_input`, `click_entity`, `hover_entity` and `sweep_pointer` refuse an `x/y/z` that is not three numbers rather than falling back to no aim or the entity's centre. Every tool that requires numbers names the offender and what arrived — `... (y arrived as string "3.0", not a number)` — so read the error rather than re-deriving it: it is telling you your argument was not a number, not that you forgot it. A whole number written `N.0` can arrive that way; write `3`, not `3.0`. **That coercion happens in your MCP client, before the request exists, so it does not reproduce over `curl` (where `3.0` stays a number) — a miscased `action` reproduces from any client, because the mistake is in the value you chose, not in how it was carried.** `action` values are lowercase and matched exactly (`primary`, `action_3` — never `PRIMARY`, `IA_PRIMARY` or `action3`), and the refusal names what arrived: `action does not accept string "PRIMARY"; the values are lowercase, one of: …`. Every enum argument answers the same way — `action`, `mode`, `stack`, `path`, `button`, `kind`, `eventType`, `severity`, `quality`. Both rejections come from the server and both echo your value, so they look alike in a transcript; tell them apart by comparing the echo with what you typed: `string "3.0"` where you wrote a number is your client's transport, `string "PRIMARY"` where you wrote `PRIMARY` is you.
- **The fan-out is either/or: an edge that lands entity-bound never also broadcasts to the scene root.** An aimed `press_input`/`click_entity`/`click_at` on a qualified target produces only the entity result; unaimed (or unqualified) edges reach the scene root instead. The suppression is per scene update: a second action whose edge lands in the same update as an entity-bound one loses its broadcast too — unreachable from your serialised calls, so only a human at the keyboard can trigger it. **The measurement trap: `inputSystem.isTriggered` cannot read the scene root in ANY form.** Without an entity it scans every entity's `PointerEventsResult`; passing `engine.RootEntity` does the exact same thing, because the root entity is `0` and the SDK's `if (entity)` guard treats it as absent (JavaScript falsy zero — same bug in `getInputCommand`). A scene must read `PointerEventsResult.get(engine.RootEntity)` directly, with a timestamp watermark, to observe the root broadcast. Check the scene's measurement before blaming the client — and beware a counter that only increments inside an active state (an "off-canvas samples" that needs a stroke to be open first), because it stays at zero both when nothing was sampled and when everything sampled missed.

## Movement

- `walk` moves relative to the camera and requires an explicit direction: pass `directionY: 1` for forward (`directionX` strafes); omitting both errors with "directionX and directionY must not both be zero". Scene `InputModifier` movement locks apply to `walk` exactly as they do to WASD — a zero-distance result inside a freeze zone proves the lock works; pass `ignoreInputModifiers: true` to escape deliberately.
- **`move_to` is a teleport and scenes notice.** It fires the scene's movement observers as a superhuman burst (e.g. `distance=16m over 0.05s avgSpeed=320m/s`), and a teleport that leaves one trigger volume and enters another can deliver `enter(B)` **before** `exit(A)` — B's exit handler then clears state A just installed, so a scene-wide `InputModifier` silently ends up cleared while the player stands inside the zone that set it. Use `walk` when the scene watches movement or trigger boundaries, and re-enter a zone on foot before trusting its state.
- Collider checks beat pixels for physics (cross-examine): `look_at` straight at the target, `walk` forward, then compare `get_player_state` positions to prove passage or blockage.

## Scene UI (`ReactEcsRenderer` panels)

- `ui_list stack:sdk` lists the interactable elements with their CRDT ids and screen rects; `ui_click`/`ui_set_text` fire the same pointer/value/submit events a user produces. A covered element fails with `blockedBy` instead of clicking through — that's the occlusion check working, not a bug.
- **A `disabled` element is refused, not written to.** `ui_set_text` on a disabled `<Input />` (or `ui_click` on a disabled element) fails naming the disabled state, because a user cannot type into or click one — the write would otherwise fire the scene's `onChange` for input the scene declared impossible. `ui_list` marks these `disabled: true`, so check the listing before blaming the tool. `force` does not override it: `force` skips the occlusion check, and there is nothing to skip on an inert element.
- **A written `<Input />` value and the box you see can disagree — trust the scene, never the pixels.** The write reaches the scene's `onChange` (logs and echo labels confirm the value), but the rendered field is a separate buffer the scene only repaints when its own `value` prop changes: fields the scene passes a `value` to display the write and then keep the *old* text after the scene clears them. Verify text entry from the scene's own channel — `get_scene_logs`, `get_entity_details`, an echo label — and do not report "the field shows X" from a screenshot.
- **`submit: true` empties the box, and that is the submit working.** The client blanks an SDK input the moment it has handed the submit to the scene — exactly what it does when a human presses Enter — so an empty field after `ui_set_text submit:true` means the value *was* delivered, not that the write was swallowed. Read the value back from the scene, never from the field, and do not go looking for a client bug here: an uncontrolled field renders a plain `ui_set_text` write fine, and nothing but a submit clears one.
- **Check hover states with `ui_hover` — it parks the pointer.** It enters the element (`stack:sdk crdtId:<id>`) and returns with the hover still resting, so the loop is `ui_hover` → screenshot / `get_scene_logs` → `ui_hover leave:true`. The hover also ends at the next `ui_hover` or any other `ui_*` gesture, whose result then carries `hoverEnded` — including a `ui_hover` refused for a cover, which leaves nothing parked (a covered element needs `force: true`, as for `ui_click`). So hovering then clicking the same element gives your scene `enter, leave, enter, down, up, leave` — a human gives `enter, down, up` and keeps hovering — and a hover count across a click is off by one. Only the addressed element is entered: a container's `onMouseEnter` needs the container's own `crdtId`. `device:true` is client UI only — refused for scene UI because the real mouse shares that pointer and would end the hover at any moment. An element removed while hovered (its panel closed) never gets a leave, so the scene's `onMouseLeave` never fires: `ui_hover leave:true` then reports `left` ending in `(the element is gone; no leave was sent)`.
- **An invisible client panel can eat your click, and its reach is not predictable from a screenshot — or from the last session.** The chat message feed's viewport is fully transparent but still takes clicks, stays active in the chat's normal state even with no messages, and sorts above scene UI, so "the screenshot shows nothing there" is not evidence of a clear pixel. How far up it reaches varies with screen size and chat state, so the same pixel can be covered in one session and clear in the next, even with the chat collapsed ("Press Enter to chat"). So do not pre-empt it: issue the plain click and read the result. A failure with `blockedBy: ".../ChatMessages/Viewport"` is the check working — a real click there focuses the chat — and the answer is to move the element, or pass `force: true` deliberately for that one call; a plain success means nothing covered the pixel this time, not that the cover is gone.
- **`click_at` cannot address UI — it rays into the 3D world** and reports the scene *entity* it hit. Clicking a UI element is `ui_click`, addressed by `crdtId` (sdk) or `path`/`id` (ugui), never by screen position. `ui_drag` is the one UI tool that takes screen coordinates. A `click_at` on a pixel covered by client UI or the scene's own UI **fails** with that cover in `blockedByUi` rather than clicking the world behind it — that is the check working, the same as `ui_click`'s; pass `force: true` only when you deliberately want the world through an overlay. When the cover is scene UI, `blockedByUi` names its `crdtId` ("the scene's UI (crdtId 598)") — feed that straight to `ui_click`; a client-UI cover names the element's path instead.
- **Feed `ui_drag` an element's `center`; never divide its `screenRect` by the screenshot size.** `screenRect` is in the client's *screen* pixels, which on a HiDPI display are far larger than the image you looked at (e.g. rects in a ~3424x1926 space while `screenshot` returns 1280x720). Every listing and UI action result states that space as `screen: {width, height}` — on every result, device path and failures included — and hands you a normalized `center` alongside the rect whenever an element was resolved — use the `center`, and when you need a point that isn't one, normalize against `screen`, not against the capture. A `screenshot` caption states the screen size too whenever it downscaled.
- **Scene UI events arrive one per drain window, so `ui_click`/`ui_drag` take several frames.** A scene element holds a single pending pointer event that the scene drains on its own cadence, so the tools wait for each event to be consumed before sending the next (enter → down → up → leave) — `onMouseDown` *and* `onMouseUp` handlers both fire. **Read `delivered` on every scene-UI `ui_click` result**: `{enter, down, up}`, each `consumed` (the scene received it), `not_declared` (the element has no handler for it — an `onMouseDown`-only tile reports `up: not_declared`, which is fine), `dropped` (another event overwrote it before the scene read it — the real mouse moving over the UI can do that) or `not_drained` (nothing reached the element). `ok` is true only when every press and release the element declares was consumed; an element declaring neither fails outright ("declares no PET_DOWN or PET_UP handler") rather than answering `ok` for a click that told the scene nothing. A `ui_drag` starting inside the scene UI is delivered to its elements semantically (press on the start element, release on the end element; result says `path: "sdk"`). An unconsumed press or release **fails** the call rather than reporting a delivery that never happened, so a success result is evidence of delivery; still verify a drag by its effect when the stakes are high.
- **`ui_drag` picks its path per call — pass `path:"sdk"` when you mean the scene's UI.** With the default `path:"auto"` it runs semantically (result `path:"sdk"`) only when the current scene has a UI panel *and* the start point picks an element on it; otherwise it replays the virtual mouse (`path:"device"`), which for a start point over scene UI means the drag goes into the 3D world instead. Byte-identical calls can take different paths seconds apart while the scene UI is still attaching or laying out. A fallback is reported: the result carries `pathReason` saying why the semantic path did not apply, and `ok:true` there means the *mouse* states were replayed, not that the UI got it — `pointerOver` on that result names what each end of the drag was actually over. `path:"sdk"` fails the call with that reason instead of dragging the world, which is what you want whenever the target is scene UI.
- **`device` belongs to `ui_click` only; `ui_drag` selects its path with `path`.** Any argument a tool does not declare is refused by name (`ui_drag has no argument 'device'; its arguments are: …`), so an unfamiliar rejection listing the tool's arguments means a key of yours belongs to a sibling tool — not that the tool is broken.
- **The device path refuses when the game would not receive device input — in the Editor that is whenever the Game view is unfocused.** `ui_click device:true`, `ui_hover device:true` and `ui_drag path:device` replay a virtual mouse/keyboard through the Input System, which hands pointer and keyboard device input to the Unity Editor while the Game view is unfocused (the normal state while you type in a terminal) and disables devices that cannot run in the background while a window is unfocused. The call then fails up front naming the cause — *the Unity Editor's Game view is not focused…* or *the automation mouse is disabled…* — instead of a misleading `not_drained` (four device clicks in one Editor session read that way before the check existed). Click into the Game view (or focus the Explorer window) and retry, or use the semantic path — the default, which does not go through the devices and is the right one unless hit-testing itself is under test.
- **`ui_click device:true` reaches scene UI too — paced, one pointer state per scene drain.** The virtual mouse moves onto the element, presses, and releases, each step held until the scene consumed the previous event (a device click fired per-frame used to land roughly one time in four, and still answered `ok`), and the result carries the same `delivered` verdict and `ok` rule as the semantic click, so a stand-in for a human's mouse click is now verifiable. Two limits: `force` skips the cover pre-check but cannot aim a device click *through* a client surface — the real hit-test routes it to the cover and the verdict reads `not_drained` — and a scene element left under the resting real mouse can see a `dropped` press. Every left click on scene UI, device or semantic, also posts one `IA_POINTER` down/up to the scene root, exactly as a human's does: expected, not a leak. `ui_drag path:"device"` still drives the world or client UI only — a per-frame drag writes more events than a scene element can hold — so a scene-UI drag is `path:"sdk"`. A device drag verifies no target: `ok` means the mouse states were replayed, so read `pointerOver: {start, end}` — it names what covered each end of the drag, or `"world"` when nothing did, plus an `info` line when both ends are `"world"`. Over the world it drags nothing and does not move the camera; since the left button is also the camera-pan binding, the same call can instead fail with "the drag panned the camera instead of dragging", and a locked cursor fails any pointer gesture up front ("pointer gestures need a free cursor") — client cursor state you don't control. Sweep a held pointer across the world with `sweep_pointer`.
- `ui_scroll`'s `dy` follows image coordinates — **positive scrolls the content down**. The result reports the offset actually achieved, so "did not move" means a clamp (already at that end, or the content does not overflow), not a broken call.

## Trigger areas

- Trigger areas fire `onTriggerEnter` immediately after `reload_scene` if the player is already standing inside one — reposition the player outside all triggers before testing enter/exit sequencing (and treat post-reload trigger logs as stale state, not gameplay).
- **Thin `TriggerArea` volumes never fire.** A 0.1 m-tall slab (a floor plate or zone marker) does not overlap the avatar capsule standing on top of it. Keep the thin box as the visual and put the trigger in a separate invisible box ~2 m tall centred at capsule height.

## Sound

`get_audio_state` is the one channel through which a sound cue can be judged at all: every audio emitter of the current scene — `AudioSource`, `AudioStream`, `VideoPlayer` — nearest to the player first, with `kind`, `source`, `state` (`not_created` while the scene has declared it but the client has not built the emitter yet, then `loading`/`ready`/`playing`/`paused`/`error`…), `playing`, `volume` (`authored` from the scene, `live` from the client — 0 while the scene is not current, and the `note` says so), `loop`, `pitch`, `spatial` with its `minDistance`/`maxDistance`, `currentTime`/`duration`, `position` and `distance`. `onlyPlaying: true` narrows it to what is sounding right now; `limit` defaults to 50. It is a snapshot of what the client runs this frame, not what the scene last asked for, so a cue fired for 0.3 s needs the read to land inside that window — or a log line beside it. A spatial emitter farther than its `maxDistance` is inaudible even while `playing`.
