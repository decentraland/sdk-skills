# Camera & movement reference

## Aiming the camera

- **`move_to`'s `lookAt*` is the unrefined production look-at** — the same one a scene's `movePlayerTo` makes, so expect the routine third-person residual — and the result reports `cameraRotationEuler` + `aimErrorDegrees`; a `warning` means a scene-controlled camera refused it, and a result with no `cameraRotationEuler` means no look-at happened. Because `walk` and `screenshot` follow the *camera*, a facing you did not confirm sends a `walk` the wrong way and can carry the player off the scene's edge (`scene: null`, "no running current scene"). Confirm `camera.rotationEuler.y` before a precise line or any leg near a parcel edge, and use the standalone `look_at` when the aim must be exact.
- `look_at` lines the third-person camera up through the avatar, so the avatar occludes exactly the thing you framed. To photograph a subject, first `move_to` a spot offset sideways from the camera→subject line, then `look_at`.
- `look_at` reports `aimErrorDegrees`: the angle still between the camera's forward and the requested point after the camera settled. ~0 is a clean aim; a large value plus a warning means a rig limit stopped it short, which is the honest answer rather than a false success. Feed real target coordinates — do not inflate `y` to compensate for a pitch you think the rig cannot reach.
- **Looking up in third person stalls well before the pitch clamp.** The clamp is about -54° (a target directly overhead reports `aimErrorDegrees` ~33 with a rig-limit warning, which is correct). But aiming up at a shallower target can also stop short, with a large `aimErrorDegrees` and the camera parked around -22° of pitch — the orbit boom swings toward the ground as it rises, collision shortens it, and that moves the ray origin under the refiner. "Back away so the angle is shallower" does not reliably fix it. For anything meaningfully above eye level, `set_camera_mode first_person` and `look_at` — it reaches -89° with `aimErrorDegrees` ~1.
- **Small residuals (4-10°) are routine in third person and usually harmless.** They come with the rig-limit warning even at modest elevations and on close, slightly-downward aims. Do not treat these as a failed aim: `click_entity`/`hover_entity`/`press_input` raycast toward the **requested world point**, not toward the reticle, so they still hit. The residual only matters when a *scene* measures the camera direction itself (aim cones, `look_at`-driven triggers) — that is where it can drop you outside a narrow cone.
- **A `deltaX`/`deltaY` unit is not a fixed number of degrees — calibrate before trusting any relative pan.** `camera_look` and `sweep_pointer` share the unit, and the same call turns several times further on one machine than another: rates run from about 3 to 16°/s per unit of `deltaX`, stable within a session across both tools. Both tools return `cameraRotationEuler`, so spend one throwaway pan measuring your own rate and size every later `deltaX` from it, rather than reusing a number from a doc or a previous session. This bites hardest where a turn must land somewhere specific — sweeping a held pointer across a surface, or panning into a scene's aim cone; pitching to sky to clear the reticle is unaffected, since it just runs into the clamp either way.
- **Reading small world-space text that is not a `TextShape` (texture-baked boards, labels no entity dump carries) needs first person.** A `TextShape` is a state read — `get_entity_details` returns its string. For the rest: at 1280×720 a board 10 m away is illegible even upscaled from the JPEG, so stand 2-4 m from it, `set_camera_mode first_person`, `look_at` the text, and capture `--png`; the third-person clamp is exactly what stops you getting close and looking up.

## Free camera

- The free camera is the fastest way to inspect a scene from many points of view: `set_camera_pose` places it at any absolute position, optionally aims it (`lookAt*`) and sets `fov`, auto-entering free mode. Repositioning while already free is instant (~200ms), so sweep a build cheaply — aerial plan view, each facade, eye-level details, interiors — capturing to disk between calls, instead of walking the player around. `look_at` also works in free mode (aims from the camera's own position), and the free camera stays put while the player moves, so you can even watch the avatar walk through the scene from a fixed vantage. Entering free from another mode blends over ~2-3s (the tool waits and reports `settled`).
- The free camera is a debug view, not what players see. To confirm the end-user experience, switch back to the real modes — `set_camera_mode` `first_person` / `third_person` / `drone_view` are exactly the cameras retail users have — and re-check framing, avatar occlusion, and interaction reach from there (e.g. verify a hover target is actually visible and clickable at player height, not just from a flattering freecam angle).

## Camera modes

- `set_camera_mode` respects scene locks and errors truthfully — check `get_player_state` → `camera.modeChangeAllowed` first; `false` inside a `CameraModeArea`/scene virtual camera is correct behavior worth verifying, not a tool failure. `screenshot` works in any mode.

## Precise navigation

- Collider-bump navigation moves the player along precise lines reliably: `look_at` a point past where you want to end up, `walk` with generous `seconds`, and let a blocking collider stop the player — the returned `endPosition` lands ~0.38m (capsule radius) short of the collider face, a deterministic waypoint for the next leg with no duration tuning. Timing legs precisely is fragile (jog ramp-up varies effective speed); overshooting into a collider is not. Only a final leg through a gap with nothing beyond it needs a tight duration, or the player runs off-parcel.
- Locomotion speeds (flat ground) vary by client by ~20%. **Averaged over a short burst** they are roughly `walk` ≈1.5 m/s, `jog` ≈7 m/s, `run` ≈8 m/s on a 1.2 s leg; the *sustained* speeds once ramped are much higher (about 2 / 10 / 12.5 m/s), so a longer leg covers far more ground than the burst average predicts — budget ~10 m/s for jog and ~12.5 m/s for run beyond ~2 s, or the leg ends clipped against a wall or off-parcel (a 2 s run crosses ~25 m, more than a 16 m parcel). To stop at a precise point no collider will stop you at, take one timed jog leg, read `endPosition`, then micro-correct with 0.5-1.2s `kind: "walk"` legs — position feedback plus slow corrections converges in 1-2 iterations where a single timed jog leg won't.
