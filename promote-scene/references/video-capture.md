# Footage for videos: where the pictures come from

The footage ladder for every video the kit cuts (footage posts, Shorts, trailers): use the highest rung that has the moment, and say in the report which rung each shot used. Editing, rendering and verifying the cut are in `{baseDir}/references/video-posts.md`.

| Rung | Source | Good for |
|---|---|---|
| 1 | Real playthroughs that already exist, indexed with `footage_index.py` | everything: the game as players meet it |
| 2 | Takes recorded from the Explorer with `capture_play.py` | the moments the recordings miss; scripted events, dialogues, a mechanic |
| 3 | Blender clips of the scene's own models (`render_shots.py -- clip`) | mood and establishing shots; nothing animates inside them |
| 4 | The kit's own stills with slow camera motion | always available; a teaser, not footage |

## The ladder

1. **Real playthroughs that already exist**, for every kind of video. Before capturing anything, ask for the footage inventory and mine it: the creator's own sessions in `promote/footage/`, playtest recordings, earlier capture sessions (`$CAPTURE_OUT`), clips or streams of real players (only with that player's permission). A real session shows the game as players meet it (their pacing, their mistakes, other people in the world), which scripted takes cannot; one recorded visit of about 13 minutes carried a whole 30-second cut (2026-11-26). Capture (rung 2) fills only the moments the recordings miss. Screen the chosen windows at 1 frame per second for what must stay out: spoilers, other players' names or chat with personal details, and anything the post's rules exclude. Captures from QuickTime, OBS, or the phone (`adb shell screenrecord`, `scrcpy --record`) all work. Index them before cutting: copy `{baseDir}/scripts/footage_index.py` to `promote/tools/` and run `python3 promote/tools/footage_index.py [<other folders>]` (not while the scene's Explorer preview is up: it writes `promote/footage/_index/`, or pass `--out` outside the scene). `INDEX.md` lists every recording (size, shape, length, fps, date) with contact sheets of one frame per second, each frame labelled with its timestamp, and a notes column kept across re-runs: note there what each recording shows and which moments are first-session. Pick `trimStart` from a sheet's timestamp; re-run after adding recordings (unchanged files are kept). Phone captures are wider than 16:9: `objectFit: cover` crops the sides, so check the subject is still in frame.
2. **Record it yourself from the Explorer with `promote/tools/capture_play.py`** (verified on The Horseman's Night, 2026-10-19: seven gameplay takes in about 15 minutes). The scene preview must run with the Explorer MCP server on (`npm run start -- --mcp --skip-auth-screen true`); follow **unity-explorer-mcp**'s intent and launch gates before starting or touching an Explorer. The script talks to that server over plain HTTP (`http://127.0.0.1:8123/unity-explorer-mcp`), so it works whether or not the `mcp__explorer__*` tools are bound in the session; it is the pre-established driver the creator can re-run, not the per-action curl loop. Copy `{baseDir}/scripts/capture_play.py` and `{baseDir}/scripts/takes.example.py` (as `takes.py`) into `promote/tools/`, rewrite `takes.py` from the scene's source (trigger rectangles, NPC spots, input actions, debug keys), then `python3 promote/tools/capture_play.py probe` (scene ready, player state, frames per second) and `… take <name>`. A take moves the player (`move_to` teleports between shots, `walk` for anything on camera, `press_input` for the scene's actions) and wraps the stretch to record in `with rec.take("name"):`. Both frame sources end as the same `$CAPTURE_OUT/<take>.mp4` (1920×1080, constant 30 fps, H.264, no audio), so the edit does not care which one made it:
   - **Screen recording, the preferred source** (`take … --screen --display N`, macOS; on Windows or Linux the creator records with OBS while the script drives the takes). Smooth motion at the display's own rate (~57 fps measured on a 1080p display, 2026-11-19; not yet measured on a live Explorer take). Before the first take:
     1. Ask the creator once to turn on **Screen & System Audio Recording** for the app the shell runs in (`open "x-apple.systempreferences:com.apple.preference.security?Privacy_ScreenCapture"`), then Quit & Reopen that app. Only they can click the toggle. Tell them the permission stays on for that app until they turn it off there.
     2. **Privacy**: the whole display is recorded, HUD and anything else on it included. Ask them to turn on Do Not Disturb and move chats, mail and private windows off that display.
     3. Explorer full-screen on a 1920×1080 display, then `probe --screen --display N` and read the frame it saves (`$CAPTURE_OUT/_raw/probe-display<N>.jpg`).
     4. After the first take, read the fps it prints; the Explorer's rate depends on the scene and GPU.

     The script's header covers the rest (Retina scaling, raw files, optional game audio via a loopback device UID). Footage is used muted; `screencapture -g` would record the microphone, not the game.
   - **Frame bursts, the fallback that needs no permission** (`take …` without `--screen`): a background thread calls the `screenshot` tool as fast as it serialises (12–14 frames/s at 1920 wide, world-only, Apple Silicon) and ffmpeg holds each frame for the time it really took, giving real-time but stepped motion (`assemble … --smooth` interpolates, better for pans than for cuts).

   Rules learned on the first scene:
   - **Look at the avatar before the first take, and ask.** Take one screenshot with the player in frame and read it: the wearables (an aura, a glowing effect, a loud outfit, a costume from another game), the nameplate, any emote still playing. Judge whether that avatar belongs in this game's footage (a horror airport is not the place for a purple particle halo). If it does not fit, stop and ask the creator what they think and whether they want to change wearables before the next shot: the avatar is in every third-person frame, the Explorer cannot swap outfits for you, and a wearable change is a session-level decision only they can make. Say what you saw, suggest the plainest fitting look, and wait for the answer before recording anything else. Ask once per session, not per take, unless they change the avatar.
   - **Write nothing under the scene folder while the Explorer is up.** The dev server rebuilds and restarts the scene on any change there, `promote/` included (`.dclignore` does not stop the watcher), and a restart mid-take brings the title card back and locks the player. Raw recordings, frames and clips go to `$CAPTURE_OUT` (default `<scene>/../capture/`); copy the clips into `promote/footage/` once all takes are done; edit `takes.py` in a scratch copy. A restart also resets session progress and re-arms once-per-session events (apparitions, cutscenes), which is how to retake one.
   - `walk` is camera-relative. Under a fixed SDK camera forward is the camera's yaw (north-east at yaw 45°), so measure one leg against `get_player_state` first. `look_at` is refused under a scene camera; `move_to` with `lookAt*` still turns the avatar, and scenes usually aim attacks where the avatar faces or last walked (a 0.12 s `walk` nudge re-aims).
   - `press_input` aimed at an empty world point (`x/y/z`, nothing clickable there) reaches the scene root: that is how global inputs go in (slash, dash, E, the title-card click, the scene's debug keys). A scene's own cheats and skips are fair game for reaching a moment (one-hit kills, "break the next seal"), but keep their overlay out of the frames.
   - Cheap targets: scripted events and cutscenes (walk into the trigger, wait), dialogues (E, then a click per line), a player walking (the trailer's "people in motion"), a parry on a telegraphed projectile (keep swinging every 0.35 s while it arrives). Hard: anything that needs enemy positions, since `list_scene_entities` returns ids, not names — airborne enemies, shield blockers, a boss phase. Use Blender clips for those and say so in the README.
   - **Vertical and close-ups.** `--vertical` encodes every clip 1080×1920. For native vertical (no crop, full sharpness) record frames, not `--screen`, from an Explorer whose window renders 1080×1920: the screenshot tool returns what the window renders (measured 2026-11-27: 1080×1920 frames at 18–20 frames/s on Apple Silicon). The window flags do not survive `npm run start -- … -- --windowed-mode --resolution …` (the Explorer drops both from the deep link), so serve the scene with `npm run start -- --no-client` and launch the client directly with `--windowed-mode --resolution 1080x1920` (the command is in `capture_play.py`'s header). `--resolution` is in rendered pixels and the window must fit its display: a Retina laptop display fits 1080×1920, a 1920×1080 monitor clamps it to 1080×810, and the window opens on the display it last used, so check the size `probe` prints before recording. Afterwards the Explorer remembers that window size: reset it, or the next normal launch opens small (`defaults write com.Decentraland.Explorer "Screenmanager Resolution Width" -int 1920`, and `Height` `1080`). `ex.camera_pose(x, y, z, look=…, fov=40)` gives a tighter free-camera close-up (the free camera's default is 60; the Explorer keeps the last fov for every later pose that omits it, so the helper always sends one). End that take with `ex.back_to_player()`.
   - Frame captures are world-only (no HUD, no letterbox bars) but in third person the avatar's nameplate stays in (crop or `zoom` it out in the edit when it matters; under a free-camera pose the player's own nameplate is not drawn, measured 2026-11-27); screen recordings include the HUD unless the Explorer was launched with `--disable-hud`; the Foundation's clean "no UI" screenshot still needs a human take with nameplates hidden.
   - Dev overlays draw into world-only captures (debug shapes, hit labels): scenes often ship with one on by default. Turn it off before recording and toggle it back only for a skip key.
   - Long fight takes are raw material, not kit files: record two minutes, pick the window from the sheet, trim that window into `promote/footage/` (`ffmpeg -ss <s> -t <len> -i take.mp4 -c:v libx264 -crf 18 …`) and keep the raw take outside the kit.
   - Pick the input cadence from the mechanic's own windows in the source (a parry that accepts any bolt within 2 m of a swing needs a swing every 0.35 s; a dialogue needs one click per line). Reading forgiveness windows beats guessing.
   - Read a contact sheet of every take before cutting (`ffmpeg … -vf "fps=1,scale=480:-1,tile=5x4"`); check the player actually moved (`ex.pos()` before and after) because a locked player records a static shot without any error.
3. **Blender clips of the scene's own models**: `$B -b -P promote/tools/render_shots.py -- clip preview <shot>` (640×360, writes `images/preview/<shot>_clip.mp4`), read two frames of it (`ffmpeg -i … -vf "select='eq(n\,0)+eq(n\,179)',tile=2x1" -vsync 0 check.png`), then `-- clip <shot>` → `promote/clips/<shot>.mp4` (1920×1080, `CLIP_SECONDS` = 6 s, EEVEE). The move is a slow push-in with a 4° orbit by default; set `CLIP_MOVES['<shot>'] = dict(orbit=…, dolly=…, rise=…)` for something else (`dolly` is a fraction of the camera distance, negative = closer). The same `SHOTS` functions and lighting are used, so nothing new to frame. Measured on Apple Silicon: a 6 s 1080p clip takes 2–3 minutes (Eeireport's kit-built rooms 130–180 s each, nine clips in 22 min sequentially); a whole-interior scene with many point lights is at the slow end, and the 360 px preview costs 40–130 s, most of it scene build and shader compile. A shot longer than `CLIP_SECONDS` can use `rate: 0.85` (slow motion) in the edit rather than a longer clip. Nothing animates inside the shot (no avatars, no particles, no tweens), so these suit mood, establishing and "the place" shots, not the mechanic itself.
4. **The kit's own stills with slow camera motion** (`images/screens/`, `images/renders/`, `images/keyart/`, `images/social/` quote cards). In-world stills (`capture_play.py still`, `{baseDir}/references/renders-and-images.md`) are real frames of the game, so prefer them to renders here. Always available, needs nothing new, and still produces a watchable post. Say so plainly in the report: this is a teaser, not footage, and the footage note remains open.
