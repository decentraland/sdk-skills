# Audience discovery — find and compare communities

A shared method for both [entries](acquisition.md#enter-from-the-game-or-the-people); only the
starting point differs:

- **From the game** (the usual entry): its promise and its [prior-art](prior-art.md) analogues
  lead to communities around the genre or the occasion.
- **From people:** with no game or group yet, or when the owner wants to choose an audience
  first, start from motives, occasions and practices.

Apply the [baseline-circle boundary](acquisition.md#turn-attraction-into-playable-choices)
to the search brief, delegated work and final comparison. By default, discover audiences beyond
Decentraland. When the owner names channels, investigate relevant external places and formats
within each, or record why that part remains unresolved. A blocked external source leaves a gap;
an easier internal DCL source does not fill it. Carry this scope into the saved recommendation.

The route is optional. Offer it once at a meaningful moment and follow the
[offer and decline rules](prior-art.md#offer-and-focus). A decline blocks nothing: continue
designing. The agent recommends; the owner chooses the audience. Communities inform the owner's
game; a market niche does not replace their idea.

Ask only for constraints that change the search: the team's capacity, the owner's interests,
language or region, and content they will not make. This is not a brief to complete. Plan and
save the pass with [research delivery](research-delivery.md); it can run in the background
while the design conversation continues. Interpret community material with
[acquisition](acquisition.md#research-people-as-well-as-games).

## Wide pass over real places

Widen from the starting point: from the game, beyond each analogue's fan group; from people,
through hobbies next to games, not only genres. Use the [search map and coverage review](prior-art.md#map-and-revisit-the-search)
to go wider rather than collect more names for the same group.

A candidate is a named place where people actually gather: a thread, server, channel, creator's
community or meetup. "Cozy gamers" is a label, not a candidate. For each place keep the link,
the date you observed it, a visible activity signal (recent posts, scheduled events, replies)
and its posting or promotion rules when visible. Note what you could not see. The number of
candidates follows the agreed depth; a deep pass might bring around ten, which is not a quota.
Keep the reason each candidate was included, and keep rejected ones with their reason.

Use only public material or material the owner supplies. The agent does not post, contact
members or moderators, join private spaces or pay for ads; any contact is the owner's.

## Look for unmet demand

Look for what people ask for and do not get: repeated requests, complaints, workarounds, mods,
house rules, and games that were abandoned or that people describe as "like X, but...". From
the game, start around its analogues: what do their players still miss? For
each lead keep the quote or observation, source, date and context, how often it appeared in
your sample, what people use today instead, and another explanation (the need is met elsewhere,
it is a joke, people would not play it). Write a bounded hypothesis: what was seen, where and
when, and what it does not show. Interest is not proven demand, and a missing game in a catalogue
is not proof of a gap.

## Games and advertising, separately

Keep two questions apart. **Competition for players:** what this group already plays, organises
or recommends for the same occasion; use [prior art](prior-art.md) for how those games work.
**Visible advertising and sponsorship:** promoted games, sponsored creators or ads seen in the
place, with your sample and time window. Little visible advertising can mean neglect, poor
economics or placements you cannot see. Real exposure, reach and member counts stay unknown
unless a source shows them; never replace them with zero or an average. Market size and payback
are business questions outside this route.

## Access and a path to the first players

For each candidate, note who organises or moderates, what the rules say about invitations,
what members would get from taking part, and how a first small group (for example around 20
players) could be invited. Mark permission as **unknown**, **proposed**, **permission-confirmed**
or **tested**. Your reading reaches proposed at most; only the owner's report of an organiser's
answer confirms permission, and only a real invitation tests it. For the steps from the
invitation into the game, use the [joining route](acquisition.md#turn-attraction-into-playable-choices).
Keep [baseline-circle](acquisition.md#turn-attraction-into-playable-choices) options outside
this candidate comparison.

## Compare and recommend

Compare candidates side by side, not only each one against a game. Useful criteria:

- fit with the owner's interests and intent, and with the game's promise when there is one;
- access and permission;
- evidence of unmet demand;
- saturation by similar games and by advertising;
- production feasibility for this team in Decentraland;
- sharing that comes from play itself.

In each cell give the evidence and a confidence (low, medium, high or unknown). Do not reduce
this to a numeric score or a gate; the owner can weigh criteria as they like. Include at least
two candidates that differ in motive or situation, not just in name. Recommend one or two with
the reason, say what would change the verdict, and keep the comparison open to the owner's choice.
Present this as research hypotheses, not a validated market.

## From observed behaviour to play

For promising candidates, connect what people do to a possible motivation, a playable action
and a moment worth sharing or inviting a friend to. Keep an alternative explanation beside it.
A short line from [player promise](player-promise.md) can make the idea testable with a listener.
Name the next cheap check: who would act, what you would observe, and what it can and cannot
show. An audience check before any build is one option; use `dcl-prototype` if installed,
or prepare a [handoff](hypothesis-log/handoff.md) for the chosen testing workflow.
A test of uptake is not a test of fun. Follow [scope and evidence](scope-and-evidence.md) for
what each check supports.

## Where results live

- Candidate tables, sources, observations and rejected candidates: `design/research/`, indexed
  through `design/references.md`.
- The chosen audience, its evidence, permission status and route: `design/acquisition.md`, as
  described in [acquisition](acquisition.md#keep-a-separate-working-document).
- `design/gdd.md`: only the selected decisions that shape the concept, with a link.

A per-candidate block can help keep records consistent: place and link, observation dates,
behaviours, unmet needs, alternatives, advertising seen, access and permission, game ideas,
confidence and the next check. Fill what is known and leave the rest out or marked unknown; it
is a shape, not required fields. After the owner chooses, continue with that community through
[acquisition](acquisition.md#enter-from-the-game-or-the-people). Unchosen candidates
stay in research and set no requirements for the chosen game.
