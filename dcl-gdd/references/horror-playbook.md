## Start with the author's kind of fear

What moment matters: approaching something unknown, surviving pursuit, making a costly choice,
or laughing with friends after a scare? Follow their image before offering concepts. Keep several
directions open when they want to brainstorm.

Here, “find the fun” means finding an experience people want to engage with; moment-to-moment
comfort is not the only goal. Ask what the player chooses during that experience. A short story
can work without an endlessly repeatable loop; examine repetition only where the design uses it.
Combat, comedy and company are compatible with horror when they serve this author's intent.

## Choose a lens

**Anticipation and agency.** What does the player expect before entering, and why do they go?
Try changing the lead-up to one encounter while keeping its outcome. Ask what they expected
and why they continued. A pause may mean curiosity, fear or unclear controls. [Grip 2019](https://frictionalgames.com/2019-10-9-years-9-lessons-on-horror/)

**Useful clarity, uncertain consequences.** Which information makes the choice understandable,
and which uncertainty makes it interesting? Try a readable risk cue alongside an unpredictable
event. Mystery does not require hiding every rule. Darkest Dungeon's authors describe making
stress visible; this is a contrasting approach, not a reason to add a stress meter.
[Bourassa and Sigman 2015](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/game-design-deep-dive-i-darkest-dungeon-s-i-affliction-system)

**Vulnerability with something to do.** What decision remains when direct confrontation is
unwise? Try an optional discovery beside a route to safety; ask why the player approached or
left it. Either choice may fit the fantasy. Removing weapons is one option, not proof of fear.
[Morin 2015](https://www.gamedeveloper.com/design/horror-in-the-making-how-red-barrels-outlasted-i-outlast-i-)

**Pressure, relief and company.** Where can someone recover, notice a clue or help a friend?
Compare sustained pressure with the same encounters separated by calmer moments. For a repeatable
experience, try another pass: what remains a decision after the surprise is known? For co-op,
observe what players actually do for each other. A game-state intensity estimate is not a fear
measurement. [Booth 2009](https://cdn.akamai.steamstatic.com/apps/valve/2009/ai_systems_of_l4d_mike_booth.pdf)

**Systems competing for attention.** Is a resource, puzzle or combat task supporting the intended
feeling or consuming attention needed for it? Try removing one competing demand in a small
variant. A better second attempt may also reflect familiarity. Amnesia's cuts were project-specific,
not a rule against mechanical depth. [Grip 2011](https://frictionalgames.com/2011-07-amnesia-post-mortem/)

## Build enough to answer this question

For route or control questions, rough geometry may suffice. For an atmosphere question, include
the cues carrying it: perhaps actual sound, light contrast, obscured sightlines and a reason to
proceed. Add only what that question needs; a finished setting is not a prerequisite.
An incomplete scene's weak atmosphere does not settle the concept.
[Grip 2017](https://frictionalgames.com/2017-04-navigating-the-space-of-game-design/)
For a concrete build–play–adjust probe, the optional `dcl-prototype` skill provides atmosphere
trials. Otherwise [hand off](hypothesis-log/handoff.md) the question, essential cues and playable
scope to the chosen builder. No handoff is required just to discuss a trial.

Fictional probe: a player brings a borrowed key to a locked boathouse while a bell sounds from
inside. Test one uncertain moment, such as recognising where the sound comes from or deciding
whether to open the door. This is an example to adapt, not a proposed game for every creator.

Use [platform-fit](platform-fit.md) for load-bearing controls and effects. For a dark enclosure,
handheld light, camera change or hidden avatar, reuse applicable documented capabilities and
resolve the remaining dependency. Check the intended effect in the target client before
calling its feel proven; documentation alone does not establish atmosphere. If the intended
experience depends on company, a solo pass cannot
settle that question. Do not force multiplayer into an intentionally solo prototype.

After play, ask neutrally: “What did you expect there?” or “What made you turn back?” Keep the
observed action, the player's explanation and your interpretation separate. Walking instead of
running does not prove understanding; a scream does not prove sustained tension. Confusion,
unreadable space or unpleasant effects are alternative explanations, not diagnoses.

An exploratory try needs no invented scare score or H-file. Save its question, build, observations
and unknowns under the existing handoff contract. A registered experiment keeps its agreed
criterion. Neither a successful build nor a narrated scene establishes the intended feeling.
