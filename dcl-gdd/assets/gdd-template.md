## Before you start — delete this whole section before submitting

- **Check the filled example first (`gdd-example.md`): that is what a finished GDD looks like** — including its length and its honest `TBD:`.
- Can't answer a question yet? Write `TBD:` — no question blocks submission. `TBD:` is a legal answer in any field. **If a field is simply not how your game works, delete it instead.**
- **Respect the provided word limits for answers.** Short writing forces clear thinking; we can always ask for more detail later.
- **A rough sketch or greybox screenshot beats polished concept art.** We fund games, not documents.
- **Anything playable is a bonus** — put the strongest link in the TL;DR.
- Write simple, direct English (B2 level). Unfamiliar platform word? See the **Glossary** at the end.
- For submission, delete this section and **everything written in italics** — guidance, word limits, program notes — plus the Glossary and unused placeholders; keep headings, field labels, tables, and your answers.

**A GDD that nails Sections 1–3, has at least a little in Sections 4–5 (return and social play), and is honest everywhere else is a legitimate first submission.**

## 2. First Minutes & How to Play

*Maximum 180 words across the Player experience cells; fixed time labels and the separate How to Play block do not count. Write in second person and present tense: "You spawn… you see… you act…" Describe what the player actually sees, does, and receives.*

| Time | Player experience |
|---|---|
| **0–5 seconds after control** | What do you **see** at spawn that tells you what to do — without mandatory reading? |
| **5–10 seconds** | What happens when you act? By 10 seconds, the player can state the immediate goal. |
| **10–60 seconds** | The first action becomes a complete micro-goal: shortest necessary hint, first reward, first optional social signal if another player is present. |
| **1–3 minutes** | First complete loop — or a smaller complete cycle with a meaningful payoff and clear outcome. |
| **3–10 minutes** | First meaningful choice or escalation, and the next visible goal. |
| **Natural stopping point** | What is completed or saved? The last thing you see before leaving should already show tomorrow's reason to return (your Section 4 hook). |

*Program bar — **the 5/10 rule**: once the first minute is playable, at least 80% of playtesters perform the first useful action within 5 seconds and can state the immediate goal within 10, without help. Untested is fine at this stage.*

- [main verb + immediate objective]
- [how progress or success works]

## 4. Why Players Come Back

*Events and Community channels can remind players, but don't rely on reminders alone: the return reason must also live in the player's memory or in their friends.*

*Explain the enjoyment now, the anticipated next visit, and what keeps play meaningful over time. A timer or appointment is an option, not a requirement.*

### 4.1 The next-day (D1) sentence

*Complete this after choosing your two hooks in Section 4.3. Point to one of them and what the player expects before returning; name timing only when the mechanism depends on it.*

> "A player who enjoyed their first session returns the next day (D1) because [Hook 1 or 2] ______, which they know or anticipate from ______."

### 4.2 The progression chain

*Then show the end of the first week as a scene: 3–4 sentences in second person, as in Section 2. If it reads the same as the first session, this section has work left to do.*

*If the game has currency or tradable rewards, add one line: how they are earned, how they are spent or removed, and the main abuse risk.*

### 4.3 Two return hooks

*Choose exactly two mechanisms. At least one must create a concrete reason to return the next day (D1). Use this menu as inspiration, or add your own — the menu is guidance: delete it before submitting.*

| Example mechanism | Return reason it is meant to create |
|---|---|
| **Appointment timer** — something finishes, respawns or unlocks at a known future time | A known completion time gives the player a concrete reason to remember. |
| **Daily goals + streak** — rotating daily tasks, escalating reward track | A visible routine and next milestone; allow recovery instead of punishing resets. |
| **Weekly leaderboard reset / small leagues** — rank inside small groups, fresh start weekly | A deadline and achievable fresh start create a weekly rhythm without requiring a new level. |
| **Named rival** — a specific player to beat, who learns who beat them back | A specific person turns a score into a relationship: "Marta beat your 47.2" is a return reason no anonymous board gives. |
| **Collection** — visible x/N progress toward a displayable reward | Visible unfinished progress creates a clear next objective; define what happens when the set is complete. |
| **Recurring scheduled event** — e.g. every Friday 20:00 UTC | A fixed time creates a shared appointment — the time itself is the return reason; an Event listing and an optional community channel only remind. |
| **Team / crew obligation** — small persistent groups with shared goals | A shared goal turns return into a social commitment: "my crew expects me." |
| **Season track** — a free time-limited progress track (6–8 weeks, running live after launch) | A deadline and fresh start create a comeback moment; align timing with program cycles and keep latecomers useful. |
| **Async traces** — things players leave behind that others discover later | Player traces connect visits when few people are online; account for storage, moderation, and expiry. |

## 5. Social by Design

***Social gaming is play whose value depends on other people being present, recognizable, and consequential.***

*Fun alone, better with friends: describe worthwhile play with nobody else present, then how another player improves it. Being technically survivable or waiting for company is not enough.*

*These are lenses on your design, not features your game must have — no game uses all of them. If a row is not how your game works, say so in one line or delete the row; `TBD:` is for answers that will come later. The filled example shows how short an answer can be.*

| | |
|---|---|
| **The repeatable social loop** | `Player A [signals or starts ___] → Player B [responds ___] → together or competitively they [achieve ___] → [shared or visible consequence] gives them a reason to regroup.` *The response must be voluntary and change another player's options, outcome, reward, status, relationship, or moment-to-moment play.* |
| **The disappearance test** | If every other player vanished tomorrow but their traces and data remained — what exactly breaks in your game? *"Nothing" is an honest answer — it means a solo game with social evidence around it; this describes social dependence, not proof of solo enjoyment or meaningful social value.* |
| **From strangers to a group** | Within 30 seconds, how does a newcomer know whom to join, what role to take, and how to begin coordinating — without voice or a shared language? |
| **Recognition & continuity** | Where does a player first learn another player's name? What persists between returning players — a role, rivalry, routine, reputation, obligation, or shared memory — and how does it change a later session? |
| **Quiet hours & player counts** | With nobody else online, a solo player enjoys ___ because of this action, response or discovery: ___. If no earlier-player traces exist yet, ___. Social play becomes viable at ___ players (your social threshold); the ideal group is ___; the target player count is ___ players. Tested count, if any: ___ (source, client/device and conditions; otherwise not tested). How does a solo arrival meet another player by design, not by luck? |
| **Drop-in / drop-out** | How does a late arrival contribute immediately? If someone leaves or tries to spoil the activity, can everyone else continue? |
| **Visible play (the bystander test)** *— one sentence* | What does someone understand by watching another player play? |
| **Shareable play (the memorable moment)** *— one sentence* | Describe one specific moment a player would screenshot or clip and share. |
| **Bring-a-friend** *— maximum 2 sentences* | At what in-game moment would a player want a specific person here — and what becomes better or different once they arrive? |

## Before you submit — self-check, delete before submitting

*The criteria and thresholds below are the program's — you do not invent a measurement framework, and the [dashboard](https://decentraland.zone/creators-data/dashboard.html) already tracks return for you. Do not plan around custom in-game tracking.*

| Criterion | Decision rule |
|---|---|
| **Immediate comprehension** | The 5/10 rule (Section 2): once playable, at least 80% perform the first useful action by 5 seconds and state the immediate goal by 10 seconds without help. |
| **Voluntary continuation** | Without prompting, players choose another loop or round, invite, regroup, or ask when they can return (Sections 2, 4). |
| **Progression and return** | Progress changes later play (Section 4); the v1 floor is >10% first-week return (dashboard D7). |
| **Intentional social play** | A voluntary action changes another player's play, options, reward, status, or relationship; joining or leaving does not break play (Section 5). |
| **Social continuity** | Returning players recognize people, roles, relationships, routines, reputation, or shared history; at least one changes a later session (Section 5). |
| **Visible, shareable play** | Nearby users can understand the activity, and play creates a specific moment worth sharing (Sections 5, 7). |
| **Discoverability Bar** | Packaging, spawn, and first-minute play promise the same experience, with no unresolved IP or Content Policy issue (Sections 1–2, 7). |

*Program-level target — above the v1 floor: **more than 20% first-week return (dashboard D7) and meeting the Discoverability Bar.***

- [ ] The first useful action needs no mandatory reading; How to Play is exactly 3 short bullets

- [ ] My D1 sentence points to one of my 2 selected hooks with a concrete trigger or timing — not "it's fun" — and progression changes a later decision, capability, status, or role
- [ ] I designed the social loop, the player counts, quiet-hour play, and drop-in / drop-out — and returning players can recognize something that persists between them

- [ ] Every core-loop verb has a touch mapping; I named my top risk and its fallback

## Glossary — reading help, delete before submitting

| Term | Meaning |
|---|---|
| **greybox / blockout** | A rough version of your space built from plain grey shapes — playable, but without final art. |
| **vertical slice** | One small part of the game brought close to final quality, to show how the whole will feel. |
| **spawn** | The exact spot and view where a player appears when they enter your scene. |
| **World** | Your own Decentraland scene at its own address, separate from the main map. |
| **Genesis City** | The main shared Decentraland map, made of LAND parcels addressed by coordinates. |
| **NAME** | A Decentraland name you claim; it gives you a World address and the right to deploy there. |
| **IP** | Intellectual property: characters, names, logos, music, or designs that someone else owns. |
| **fps** | Frames per second — how smoothly the scene runs; higher is smoother. |
| **client** | The app used to play Decentraland — the desktop or mobile version. |
| **hook** | A mechanic that gives the player a concrete reason to come back — e.g. a daily goal or an appointment timer. |
| **pillar** | One of the 2–3 short phrases that define your experience — the things you never cut. |
| **scope** | Everything you commit to build; scope pressure is the temptation to build more than v1 needs. |
| **playtest** | Watching real players (playtesters) try your scene and noting what they do. |
| **fallback** | Your plan B if the first choice fails or is unavailable. |
| **power gap** | When long-time players are so far ahead that a new player cannot compete or contribute. |
| **retention** | How well your experience brings players back after their first visit. |
| **D1** | The share of new players who come back the next day. |
| **dashboard D7** | First-week return: the share of new players who come back at least once during days 1–7, as shown on the [program dashboard](https://decentraland.zone/creators-data/dashboard.html). |
